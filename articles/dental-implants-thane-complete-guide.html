Dental Implants in Thane: The Complete Guide

Everything you need to know before replacing a missing tooth with a dental implant.

Missing a tooth changes how you eat, speak and smile — and the longer a gap is left, the more it can affect the surrounding teeth and jawbone. Dental implants have become the most reliable, long-term way to replace a missing tooth, and more patients in Thane are choosing them over bridges or removable dentures. This guide walks through what a dental implant actually is, how the procedure works, and what to expect from start to finish.

What Is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that is placed into the jawbone to act as an artificial tooth root. Over a few months, the bone fuses with the titanium in a process called osseointegration, creating a stable foundation. Once healed, a custom-made crown is fitted on top, giving you a replacement tooth that looks, feels and functions like your own.

Types of Dental Implants

Single tooth implants replace one missing tooth without touching the teeth next to it. Implant-supported bridges use two or more implants to replace several missing teeth in a row. For patients missing most or all of their teeth, All-on-4 or All-on-6 implants anchor a full arch of replacement teeth on just four to six implants, often in a single visit.

The Step-by-Step Procedure

Every implant treatment starts with a consultation and a 3D scan to check bone density and plan exact implant placement. The implant is then placed under local anaesthesia — most patients describe the sensation as pressure rather than pain. Over the next three to six months, the implant fuses with the jawbone. Once healing is confirmed, an abutment and a custom-shaded crown complete the restoration.

Why Choose an Implant Over a Bridge or Denture

Unlike a bridge, an implant doesn't require grinding down the healthy teeth next to the gap. Unlike a removable denture, it doesn't shift while eating or speaking, and it actively preserves the jawbone underneath, preventing the sunken look that comes with long-term tooth loss. With proper care, dental implants routinely last 15 to 25 years or longer.

Are You a Good Candidate?

Most healthy adults with adequate jawbone density are good candidates for dental implants. Patients with lower bone volume can often still qualify after a bone graft. Conditions like uncontrolled diabetes or heavy smoking can affect healing, so your dentist will review your medical history during the initial consultation.
