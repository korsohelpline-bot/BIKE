Dental Implants vs. Dental Bridge: Which Is Right for You?

Comparing the two most common ways to replace a missing tooth.

When you're missing a tooth, the two most common permanent solutions are a dental implant or a dental bridge. Both restore your smile and your bite, but they work very differently — and that difference matters over the next 10, 15 or 20 years.

How a Dental Bridge Works

A traditional bridge replaces a missing tooth by anchoring a false tooth to the two healthy teeth on either side of the gap. Those adjacent teeth are filed down to hold crowns that support the bridge. It's a faster, often cheaper fix, but it depends on the strength of neighbouring teeth.

How a Dental Implant Works

A dental implant replaces the missing tooth root directly with a titanium post placed in the jawbone, topped with an independent crown. It doesn't rely on or alter the teeth next to it, and it stimulates the jawbone the way a natural tooth root would.

Durability and Long-Term Value

A well-placed dental implant can last 15 to 25 years or longer with good oral hygiene. A dental bridge typically needs replacement every 10 to 15 years, and if a supporting tooth develops decay or fails, the whole bridge may need to be redone.

Impact on Jawbone and Nearby Teeth

Because a bridge doesn't replace the tooth root, the jawbone under the gap can gradually shrink over time. An implant preserves bone density because it functions like a natural root. A bridge also requires healthy teeth to be reduced in size, which an implant avoids entirely.

Which Option Costs Less Upfront

A bridge usually has a lower upfront cost and shorter treatment time, making it appealing for patients who want a faster fix or who aren't candidates for implant surgery. An implant costs more initially but is typically more cost-effective over a 15 to 20 year horizon.
